Disease: Common Cold
Medical Specialty: Infectious Diseases

Source:
World Health Organization clinical guidance

Overview:
The common cold is a viral infection affecting the upper respiratory tract. It is generally a self-limiting condition.

Cause:
The common cold can be caused by several viruses, including rhinoviruses.

Transmission:
The common cold can spread through respiratory droplets and close contact with infected people.

Common Symptoms:
- Runny nose
- Blocked or stuffy nose
- Sneezing
- Sore or irritated throat
- Cough
- Mild fever
- Mild body or muscle aches

Typical Course:
Common cold symptoms generally improve without antibiotic treatment. Persistent or worsening symptoms may require medical evaluation because other respiratory illnesses or complications may have similar symptoms.

Possible Complications:
- Sinus-related complications
- Ear infections
- Lower respiratory infections such as pneumonia

Diagnosis:
The common cold is generally identified from the clinical symptoms. Routine laboratory testing is usually not required for an uncomplicated cold.

Management:
Management is generally supportive and focuses on relieving symptoms, maintaining hydration and getting adequate rest.

Important Note:
Antibiotics do not treat viral infections such as the common cold. Persistent, severe or unusual symptoms should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.
