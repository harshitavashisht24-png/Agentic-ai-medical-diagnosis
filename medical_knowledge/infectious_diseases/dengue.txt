Disease: Dengue
Medical Specialty: Infectious Diseases

Source:
World Health Organization (WHO)
Dengue and Severe Dengue Fact Sheet
Updated: 21 August 2025

Overview:
Dengue is a viral infection caused by the dengue virus. It is transmitted to humans mainly through the bites of infected mosquitoes. Dengue is common in tropical and subtropical regions.

Cause:
Dengue is caused by dengue virus (DENV).

Transmission:
Dengue is mainly transmitted through the bites of infected female Aedes mosquitoes, particularly Aedes aegypti.

Common Symptoms:
- High fever
- Severe headache
- Pain behind the eyes
- Muscle pain
- Joint pain
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Rash
- Swollen glands

Symptoms usually begin several days after infection and commonly last for several days.

Severe Dengue / Warning Signs:
- Severe abdominal pain
- Persistent vomiting
- Rapid breathing
- Bleeding gums or nose
- Blood in vomit or stool
- Extreme fatigue
- Restlessness
- Excessive thirst
- Pale and cold skin
- Feeling weak

Severe symptoms require immediate medical evaluation.

Risk:
People who have previously had dengue infection may have a higher risk of severe dengue during a subsequent infection.

Diagnosis:
Clinical evaluation and appropriate laboratory testing may be required to confirm dengue infection.

Prevention:
- Avoid mosquito bites.
- Use appropriate mosquito-control measures.
- Reduce mosquito breeding sites.
- Use protective measures against mosquitoes, particularly in areas where dengue is common.

Treatment:
There is no specific antiviral treatment for dengue. Management focuses on appropriate supportive medical care, monitoring and early recognition of severe dengue.

Important Note:
This information is intended for educational and research purposes. It does not provide a confirmed diagnosis or individualized treatment recommendation.
