Disease: Influenza
Medical Specialty: Infectious Diseases

Source:
World Health Organization (WHO)
Influenza (Seasonal) Fact Sheet
Updated: 28 February 2025

Overview:
Seasonal influenza, commonly called the flu, is an acute respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses. It occurs worldwide and spreads easily between people.

Cause:
Influenza is caused by influenza viruses.

Transmission:
Influenza can spread when an infected person coughs or sneezes. Infectious droplets can reach people nearby. The virus can also spread through contaminated hands.

Common Symptoms:
- Sudden onset of fever
- Cough, usually dry
- Headache
- Muscle and joint pain
- Severe tiredness or malaise
- Sore throat
- Runny nose

The cough can sometimes continue for two weeks or more.

Incubation:
Symptoms generally begin around two days after infection, with a typical range of approximately 1–4 days.

Risk:
Influenza can cause severe illness, especially in people at higher risk and people with certain underlying medical conditions.

Complications:
Severe influenza can lead to complications including pneumonia and sepsis.

Diagnosis:
Most influenza cases are diagnosed clinically. Laboratory testing may be useful in situations where influenza activity is low or when other respiratory infections produce similar symptoms.

Prevention:
- Influenza vaccination is an important prevention measure.
- Cover the mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing.
- Wash hands regularly.
- Avoid close contact when experiencing symptoms.

Treatment / Management:
Most people recover without specific treatment. Supportive care may include rest and adequate fluid intake. People with severe symptoms or higher-risk conditions should seek medical care.

Important Note:
This information is intended for educational and research purposes. It does not provide a confirmed diagnosis or individualized treatment recommendation.
