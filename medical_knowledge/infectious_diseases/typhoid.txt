Disease: Typhoid
Medical Specialty: Infectious Diseases

Source:
World Health Organization (WHO)
Typhoid Fact Sheet
Updated: 30 March 2023

Overview:
Typhoid fever is a potentially life-threatening infection caused by the bacterium Salmonella Typhi.

Cause:
Typhoid fever is caused by Salmonella Typhi bacteria.

Transmission:
Typhoid is usually transmitted through contaminated food or water. After ingestion, the bacteria can multiply and spread into the bloodstream.

Common Symptoms:
- Prolonged fever
- Fatigue
- Headache
- Nausea
- Abdominal pain
- Constipation
- Diarrhoea
- Rash in some patients

Severe Disease:
Severe cases may result in serious complications and can be life-threatening.

Risk Factors:
Risk is higher in populations with limited access to safe drinking water and adequate sanitation. Children are also an important high-risk group.

Diagnosis:
Typhoid fever can be confirmed through blood testing.

Prevention:
- Access to safe drinking water.
- Maintain good sanitation.
- Follow good food and hand hygiene.
- Typhoid vaccination can help prevent infection.

Treatment:
Typhoid fever can be treated with antibiotics. Increasing antimicrobial resistance can make treatment more complicated, so treatment should be determined by a qualified healthcare professional.

Important Note:
This information is intended for educational and research purposes. It does not provide a confirmed diagnosis or individualized treatment recommendation.
