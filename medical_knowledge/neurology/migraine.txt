Migraine and Other Headache Disorders
Updated: 24 October 2025

Overview:
Migraine is a primary neurological headache disorder characterized by recurring attacks. Attacks commonly last several hours and may continue for up to 72 hours.

Common Symptoms:
- Moderate or severe headache
- Pulsating headache
- Headache that may occur on one side or behind the eye
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Sensitivity to light
- Sensitivity to sound

Migraine attacks may be aggravated by routine physical activity.

Aura:
Some people experience an aura before or during a migraine attack. Aura can involve temporary visual, sensory or other neurological symptoms.

Possible Triggers:
Possible migraine triggers can include:
- Alcohol
- Sleep disruption
- Certain foods
- Other individual triggers

Risk and Pattern:
Migraine often begins around puberty and can occur throughout life. It is more common in women and can also affect children and adolescents.

Diagnosis:
Migraine is diagnosed through appropriate clinical assessment and recognition of the characteristics of the headache and associated symptoms.

Management:
Management can involve appropriate medical treatment, lifestyle measures and education about migraine triggers. Regular sleep, exercise, hydration and identifying personal triggers can be useful components of management.

Important Note:
This information is intended for educational and research purposes. It does not provide a confirmed diagnosis or individualized treatment recommendation.
