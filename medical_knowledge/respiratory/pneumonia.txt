Disease: Pneumonia
Medical Specialty: Respiratory

Source:
World Health Organization (WHO)
Pneumonia information

Overview:
Pneumonia is an acute respiratory infection affecting the lungs. During pneumonia, the air sacs in the lungs can fill with fluid or pus, making breathing difficult and limiting oxygen intake.

Causes:
Pneumonia can be caused by:
- Viruses
- Bacteria
- Fungi

Common Symptoms:
- Cough
- Shortness of breath
- Fever
- Sweating
- Chills
- Fatigue
- Chest pain
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhoea
- Confusion, especially in older adults

Risk Factors:
People at increased risk can include:
- Young children
- Older adults
- People with weakened immune systems
- People with certain pre-existing health conditions
- People exposed to indoor air pollution
- People living in crowded conditions

Diagnosis:
Diagnosis is based on clinical assessment and relevant medical evaluation. Symptoms can overlap with other respiratory conditions.

Severe Disease:
Severe pneumonia can cause significant breathing difficulty and may require hospital care.

Prevention:
- Vaccination can help prevent some causes of pneumonia.
- Maintain good hand hygiene.
- Reduce exposure to indoor air pollution.
- Avoid smoking.
- Maintain adequate nutrition.

Treatment:
Treatment depends on the cause and severity. Bacterial pneumonia may require antibiotics prescribed by a healthcare professional. Severe pneumonia may require hospitalization and additional supportive care.

Important Note:
This information is intended for educational and research purposes. It does not provide a confirmed diagnosis or individualized treatment recommendation.
